There are several types
Two parts to a speech act:
- locutionary act 
	- The act of simply uttering a sentence, a description of what the speaker says. Typically using a referring expression and a predicating expression to express a proposition. 
	- “You must stop smoking” - “you” is the referring expression and “stop smoking” is the predicating expression 
- Illocutionary act 
	- What the speaker does in uttering the sentence
	- Acts like stating, requesting, promising, apologizing, commanding
	- There are several types:
		- Representative
			- “I have five toes on my right foot.”
			- stating, asserting, denying, confessing, admitting, notifying, concluding, predicting
		- Directive
			- “Shut the door”
			- requesting, ordering, forbidding, warning, advising, suggesting, insisting, recommending
		- Question
			- it’s a question you dummy
		- Commissive - a commitment 
			- “I’ll meet you at the library at 10:00 PM”
			- promising, vowing, volunteering, offering, guaranteeing, pledging, betting
		- Expressive - an expression of emotional state
			- “I’m sorry I called you a dweeb”
			- apologizing, thanking, congratulating, condoling, welcoming, deploring, objecting, and so on
		- Declaration - changing the status of some entity
			- “You’re out”
			- appointing, naming, resigning, baptizing, surrendering, excommunicating, arresting
