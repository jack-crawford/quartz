Franks Casket - 8th century, Northumbria - whalebone box about the size of a shoebox
- “a well-conceived program of storytelling”, combining scenes from norse, germanic, and christian stories and histories
- on the front, the left panel is the story of Weland, and the right is the Magi offering their gifts to baby Jesus
- Weland - crazy story (after being hamstrung to be a captive smith for the king, turns the king’s sons into goblets, impregnates his daughter, and flies away on wings that he’s made himself from feathers)
- the Magi are labeled, Weland is not
- “The flood cast up the fish on the mountain-cliff. The terror-king became sad where he swam on the shingle. ” - riddle inscribed on the box
	- the answer is whale bone - the box is the punchline
- Borges made a pilgrimage to the British Museum to see this, to pick it up
- Romulus and Remus are on one side
- Sacking of the second temple of Jerusalem on the other

## Week 2 - Iceland
Norwegian explorers found Irish hermits and monks in Iceland and once the Nords spoiled their solitude, the hermits left (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papar) 
Practically independent region but technically under the king of Norway
Althing - established in 930, still in existence
Oldest and longest-lived legislative body, convening in summers
Godi - chieftains 
Status, honor, love, jealousy are the chief social concerns, not wealth or opulence 
*Laxardal* was written in 13th century

**How did this society maintain justice without central authority?**
No capital punishment — outlawry was the worst punishment that could be sentenced 
Outlawry means you are outside the law, unprotected from any recourse, revenge, or assault
